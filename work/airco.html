Brand Identity Voice & Tone Brand Book Naming Luxury

Air Co.
Almost Impossible.

Brand launch campaign · 2019

“You’re holding a bottle of the future.”
Air Co., brand book.

The challenge.

Air Co. had invented a way to pull carbon dioxide out of the atmosphere and turn it into alcohol. The first product was vodka, a carbon-negative premium spirit, with beauty and fragrance to follow.

The brand had to recruit engineers and sell a premium spirit to luxury buyers, two audiences with nothing in common. Told plainly, the story sounded like a science fair project to one and a sustainability pitch to the other.

My Role

  • Concept and tagline
  • Manifesto
  • Brand voice and identity
  • Full site copy
  • Social posts
  • Brand book

Result

  • Fast Company Top 10 Product Launches of 2019
  • Sold in Michelin-starred restaurants
  • Covered across design, food, tech, and sustainability press

The insight.

Both audiences had the same first reaction: that can’t be real.

To an engineer, refining carbon from the air into something pure sounded like a claim that wouldn’t make it out of the lab. To someone ordering a martini, vodka made from air sounded like a gimmick. Rather than argue with that reaction, I made it the brand.

The idea.

“Almost Impossible.”

The line says what everyone is already thinking, then lets the product prove otherwise. An engineer hears a hard problem, a buyer hears something to say while pouring, and the bottle starts to feel rare, which a sustainability claim rarely manages.

How it traveled.

The manifesto takes two sentences to get to carbon and never mentions vodka.

Air Co., manifesto

We believe in solving problems, the harder the better.

We know that the best solutions aren’t always the most obvious, but smart executions of the right science can literally change the world.

So we tackled the planet’s most vexing problem and turned a negative to a positive: we invented a way to capture excess carbon from the air and turn it into ultra-refined, covetable products.

Things we’re proud to own and pleased to share.

Goods that do good.

On the bottle, the idea becomes a conversation piece for whoever’s holding it.

Bottle back label

“Vodka made from air, water & sun. The bottle you’re holding contains the world’s most sustainable vodka, and we make it out of thin air. How’s that possible? …”

Site and social worked the same way, with the improbable fact first and the product second. The voice treated the carbon problem as serious and the vodka as a drink.

The brand book.

Air Co. was already expanding into beauty and fragrance, so I wrote a brand book whose every rule had to hold for products that didn’t exist yet. Whoever wrote the fragrance launch would have to do it without me.

The outcome.

Fast Company named it one of the top ten product launches of 2019. The coverage ran across design, food, tech, and sustainability press, and Michelin-starred restaurants carried it.
