The mandate.

Lincoln was a premium brand running on mass-market rules: louder websites, more features, more noise. Every competitor was doing the same thing, and the category couldn't hear itself anymore.

I made the opposite argument: luxury isn't visual, it's physiological. You don't tell someone that a car is calm. You make the writing calm and let them feel it.

It's an easy point to make in a deck and a harder one to hold across five years of launches. Most of the job was making restraint defensible in rooms where subtlety reads as underdelivering.

What I built.

The voice.

I owned the New York side of the brand voice system that governed every line of Lincoln copy for five years: .com, product launches, campaigns, CRM. Built with the Detroit team and held across both offices. Fewer adjectives. More silence. Sentences that sound the way the vehicles feel.

The system's job was never to make my writing better. A voice system works when a junior writer can make the right call on a CRM subject line without hesitating.

The architecture.

I led the rebuild of the Lincoln.com experience around presence instead of performance specs. We cut the feature tables. We led with photography, breath, and restraint.

Cutting spec tables from a car website isn't a copy decision, it's a business argument, and it gets made against people whose job is comparison shopping. SEO and strategy had reservations. We pushed for experience over feature lists and won.

The platform.

I concepted and wrote the launch of Lincoln Rejuvenate™, the brand's in-vehicle wellness experience. The naming, the voice, and the digital campaign.

Take a seat. It'll take it from here.

Naming a category the customer has never heard of is the part of the job with the least cover, because there's no benchmark to point at when someone asks whether it's right. Rejuvenate was an in-vehicle experience, and mirroring it on a desktop screen was a conceptual leap the client worried would diminish the thing itself. We answered with prototypes: enough care in the visual and verbal rendering that it transported the user anyway. That's what sold it.

The cultural layer.

Two platforms that took Lincoln's voice past owned surfaces.

Redeem Your Dream. I led the writing: the name, the platform line, campaign voice, social, and .com expressions. The work extended Lincoln Access Rewards from a points ledger into culture, wellness, and adventure, and moved the brand from the showroom into ownership.

100 Faces of Lincoln. A year-long creator platform: 100 owners, their stories in their words. I shaped the voice and editorial framework the stories were built on, working with the Hudson Rouge team.

52M impressions. 7.6% engagement. Silver Shorty Honor.

Hundreds of digital experiences. Dozens of launches. One language across every surface Lincoln has.

The work that started as digital copy became the brand's voice. The voice became the experience. The experience moved the business.

Lincoln didn't need a louder voice. It needed a cleaner one, and it needed everyone writing it to agree on what clean meant. That's what I built.
