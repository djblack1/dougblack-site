The script was a list of questions.

Pantone Connect puts 15,000 Pantone colors in a designer’s phone, browser, and Adobe apps. It is a utility, and the easy way to launch a utility is to explain its features. We handed it to three working studios in New York instead: Hillary Taymour of Collina Strada, Matt Pecina of Studio Guapo, and Joe Perez and Haris Fazlani of WØRKS.

I interviewed each of them before I wrote anything. People who are not actors sound stiff reading lines, so the scripts were open questions built from those conversations, with a short list of points every film had to reach: where the color comes from, what happens to it in the design, and how it survives production.

Every film ended on a question.

It was November 2021, so the launch was a live stream. Viewers recorded a video question for the artist they had just watched, and the artists answered them on air.

I wrote everything that happened in the broadcast, including the remarks from Pantone’s leadership. The same three stages ran through the launch emails, the paid and organic social, and the microsite, so the whole launch said one thing in every format.

This is the method I used on American Express eight years earlier: start with what real people say about the product, and build the campaign out of that. A credit card and a color tool have nothing else in common.
