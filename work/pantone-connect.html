Launch event, streamed live

The script was a list of questions.

Pantone Connect puts 15,000 Pantone colors in a designer’s phone, browser, and Adobe apps. It is a utility, and the easy way to launch a utility is to explain its features. This launch handed it to three working studios in New York instead: Hillary Taymour of Collina Strada, Matt Pecina of Studio Guapo, and Joe Perez and Haris Fazlani of WØRKS.

I interviewed each of them before I wrote anything. People who are not actors sound stiff reading lines, so the scripts were open questions built from those conversations, with a short list of points every film had to reach: where the color comes from, what happens to it in the design, and how it survives production.

Every film ended on a question.

Viewers recorded a video question for the artist they had just watched, and the artists answered them live during the broadcast. The films were the opening of a conversation, so the scripts had to leave room for one.

One idea, every format.

The same three stages ran through everything else I wrote for the launch: the email series, the paid and organic social, the microsite, and the script for the live broadcast, including the remarks from Pantone’s leadership.

This is the method I used on American Express eight years earlier: start with what real people say about the product, and build the campaign out of that. A credit card and a color tool have nothing else in common.
