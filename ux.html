When AI writes the copy.

For much of my career the job was writing the line. When a model writes the line, the job becomes writing the rule underneath it, and then deciding whether what came out is any good. Those are two disciplines. I do both.

Writing the rules. A voice guideline written for a person and one written for a model are never the same. A person reads a tone attribute and interprets it.

On Lincoln, I built the brand's voice and style rules into a working model as an instruction set, with a strict and growing list of guardrails, so product owners who were not writers could draft on brand. Nothing reached the client without a writer reading it first. I did the same at AREA15, where no voice guide existed at all, so the rules and the instruction set were written at the same time.

Grading the output. I train and evaluate AI models on writing quality for an AI company, under NDA. That means sitting with generated text and deciding, line by line, what separates fluent from good, with the reasoning written out. Doing both sides changed how I write standards, and it is why the guidelines get rewritten every time the model changes.

A rule precise enough for a model to follow exposes every place the human version was vague and got away with it.

How I got here.

I started as a journalist, then I moved to brand copywriting. Along the way, the work shifted: the copy that mattered most was no longer the flashy ad. It was the button no one noticed and the onboarding step no one dreaded. The brands that take that seriously are the brands I want to work with.

The work.

SoundCloud Go

Music streaming  ·  2015–2017
Role: First dedicated writer at the company. Brand voice, subscription product copy, and launch.

A paid tier launched to 175 million people who had never paid for anything. The first version failed. I wrote the second.

The first launch led with everything the subscription included and put the price further down, so it read as the free product being taken away and sold back. The audience said so immediately. I argued internally for inverting it, free stated plainly and first, subscription as an upgrade on top, and wrote that version. It replaced the original and it is still what SoundCloud runs.

Better.com

Mortgage  ·  2020–2021
Role: Embedded product writer across UX, brand, and product copy, in Figma alongside design, product, and legal.

One of the most anxiety-inducing processes in American life, moved entirely online.

I spent a year embedded across onboarding flows, loan applications, error states, email sequences, and the brand voice system that governed how to sound like Better on every surface. Error and delay states are the hardest copy in a mortgage flow. The customer is already worried, the news is rarely good, and the line has to stay calm without lying to them.

Goldman Sachs

Private wealth management  ·  2018–2020  ·  NDA
Role: Voice architecture, tone guidelines, and copy standards for a platform serving clients with $10M or more in investable assets.

Language for a design system used by teams I would never meet.

I led the copy on the language layer of Goldman's public design system, launched February 2020: labels, empty states, and error messages written once and then used by teams I would never meet. The test was never whether the lines were good. It was whether a product manager on a deadline, with no writer available, would make the same call I would. I wrote the system's principles too, as titled imperatives rather than adjectives, including one called "Consistent, not creaky."

Role: Voice, tone, and language system across VerizonWireless.com and the MyVerizon app. Dynamic headlines were specified as patterns rather than finished lines, so the system could assemble them against whatever plan the customer already had.
Webby Award Honoree, Mobile Sites and Apps, Services and Utilities, 2017

One vocabulary for 113 million people managing their own accounts.

One of the highest-traffic consumer websites in the United States. The existing site had accumulated years of copy written by different teams to different conventions. I replaced that accumulation with a system: vocabulary, nomenclature standards, and copy patterns governing navigation, product pages, onboarding, support, account management, and transactional messaging. Nobody opens a carrier app for pleasure. They open it to do one thing, and the writing either gets them through it or it does not.
